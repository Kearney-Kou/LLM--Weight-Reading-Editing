# Predictions and settings: fourth-version supplementary experiment (training longer at lr 1e-4)

Written at about 2026-11-04 09:05 UTC (as given by the time the file was saved to the Mac; see the fourth-round supplement in `4_实验结果.md`), before running any training or evaluation.
This is a **post hoc** experiment: all results of B2 were already known when it was designed. On the network trained 2,000 steps at lr 1e-4 (new AdamW state), the gain of H1 is 0.29–0.54 times that on the subject, and the far row norm dropped by only 2.9%.
The question to answer (item 1 in the first section of the third-version review): with some more training, will optimization "remove" the damage caused by the far weights by itself? That is, will the gain of H1 keep shrinking to 0?

## Settings

- The training script `代码/train_reference_lowlr_long.py` is exactly the same as `代码/train_reference_lowlr.py`:
  - starting from the subject network, new AdamW, lr 1e-4, weight decay 0.01, batch 1024;
  - `torch.manual_seed(0)`, 2 threads, the same `randperm` minibatch order.
  - The only difference: train to **10,000 steps** (i.e. +8,000 steps), saving checkpoints `网络/reference_lr1e-4_long_step{N}.npz` at steps 2,000, 4,000, 6,000, 8,000, 10,000.
  - No existing network file is overwritten.
- Consistency check: compare the step-2,000 checkpoint with the existing `reference_lr1e-4_plus2000steps.npz`. Report the maximum absolute difference, and whether the per-utterance counts of correct frames on B and C agree.
  - The hardware and torch version differ, so it may not be bitwise identical; if it is not, this is reported as it is, and the newly run step 2,000 is taken as the starting point of this experiment.
- Evaluation: exactly the same as `noise_test.py`. For each checkpoint, evaluate three versions: as is, H1 (with compensation), H1 (without compensation).
  - Compute per-utterance counts of correct frames on B and C;
  - the difference is H1(checkpoint) − the checkpoint itself;
  - bootstrap by utterance and by speaker, 2,000 times each, rng seed 0.
- Far floor: mean row norm at |Δt| 30…38, relative to the subject network (1.2718). Also report the change at |Δt| ≤ 10 and the offsets selected by H1.
- Amount of training: 10,000 steps × 1,024 = 10,240,000 frames, about 0.67 epochs. Together with the subject's 0.63 epochs, about 1.3 epochs in total.

## Predictions (my expectations)

1. **Far floor**: by step 10,000, the change relative to the subject is between −10% and 0. My guess is about −4% to −7%:
   - AdamW's weight decay shrinks it by only about 1% in 10,000 steps at lr 1e-4;
   - by step 2,000 it has already dropped 2.9%, which shows the gradient pulls these weights back a little, weakly, but slowly.
2. **Gain of H1**: keeps shrinking with the number of steps, but is still positive at step 10,000. My guess is that the version with compensation gives +0.05 to +0.15 pp on B and C, with 95% intervals excluding 0. Confidence about six in ten.
3. The accuracy of the checkpoints themselves keeps rising with the number of steps.

## Decision rules fixed in advance (they decide how the paper is written)

Look at step 10,000, the two versions (with/without compensation) and the two test sets (B, C), 4 cells in total:

- **"Optimization did not remove the obstacle within this training"**: the 95% by-utterance intervals of all 4 cells exclude 0 (and are positive).
  - The paper says: after 10,000 further steps at lr 1e-4 (about 0.67 epochs), deleting the far weights still gives a positive gain.
  - This conclusion holds only up to this amount of training, and is not generalized to "no training can remove it".
- **"Optimization will eventually remove this damage too"**: the point estimates of all 4 cells are ≤ +0.02 pp, or the intervals all include 0.
  - The paper says: the edit merely grabbed early what longer low-learning-rate training would also obtain; the conclusion is narrowed accordingly.
- All other cases are recorded as "inconsistent results", and the trend of the gain with the number of steps is reported cell by cell.

All checkpoints and all cells are reported, without selection.
