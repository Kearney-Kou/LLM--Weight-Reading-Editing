## Analysis 1: radial/tangential decomposition of the displacement (script `代码/displacement.py`)

### Settings
- **Far weights**: all rows of W1 with |Δt| ∈ 30…38 (18 offsets × 28 features × 1,024), concatenated into one vector w0 (subject network). The corresponding vector of network N is denoted w, and the displacement d = w − w0.
- **Decomposition**:
  - radial component r = (w0·d)/|w0|; a negative value means moving towards 0;
  - tangential component t = sqrt(|d|² − r²);
  - both converted to "per-weight" quantities: divided by √(number of elements).
- **Share of weight decay**: AdamW's decoupled decay over T steps, at lr η and wd 0.01, multiplies the weights by (1 − 0.01η)^T. The radial displacement computed from this serves as a reference (about −1% × |w0| at lr 1e-4 and 10,000 steps).
- **Controls**:
  - the weights near the centre, |Δt| ≤ 10, with the same decomposition;
  - row-level analysis: compute the radial component of each row (1,024 dimensions) separately, and report the fraction of rows moving towards 0.
- **Networks**:
  - the 5 checkpoints of the long lr 1e-4 training (steps 2,000–10,000);
  - lr 1e-4 same state +2,000;
  - lr 1e-3 further training +2,000, +4,000.
- **Random-walk reference**: Adam's update per step is about η·m̂/√v̂. If a weight's gradient is zero-mean noise, the momentum only smooths the noise, and the accumulated sum is still about η times the sum of T unit-variance random numbers, so the RMS displacement is about η√T. This is the expected value for pure noise; if the gradient pulls back, the actual displacement will be smaller.

### Predictions
1. lr 1e-4, step 10,000, far weights:
   - the radial component is negative (towards 0), about −0.002 to −0.004 per weight, at least 3 times the weight-decay share (about −0.0004);
   - the tangential component is about 0.010 to 0.011 per weight, at least 2 times the absolute value of the radial component.
   - This item follows almost entirely from the known norm and displacement; it mainly makes the reviewer's rough calculation formal.
2. In the long lr 1e-4 training, the far RMS displacement grows approximately as √T with the number of steps: the ratio of step 10,000 to step 2,000 is between 1.8 and 2.7 (pure random walk gives √5 ≈ 2.24, constant-velocity drift gives 5), and the magnitude is comparable to η√T (η√T = 0.010 at step 10,000).
3. Row level: at lr 1e-4, step 10,000, more than 60% of the far rows move towards 0.
4. Further training at lr 1e-3:
   - the RMS displacement is below the η√T upper bound (the upper bound is 0.045 at +2,000 steps). If it really did a random walk at this upper bound, the norm would grow by about 50%, whereas it was measured to grow by only 6%;
   - the radial component also points towards 0.
   - Less confident about this one.

### Decision rules (they decide the wording of the paper)
Look at the far weights at lr 1e-4, step 10,000:
- radial < 0, |radial| ≥ 3 × weight-decay share, and |radial| < 0.5 × tangential: write "there is a weak pull towards 0, but it is dominated by random motion" (the wording suggested in the fourth-version review).
- |radial| ≥ 0.5 × tangential: write "there is a clear pull", and revise the claims of the "accumulation version" accordingly.
- |radial| < 3 × weight-decay share: write "no pull beyond weight decay can be detected".

One more: if the displacement ratio (step 10,000 / step 2,000) is in [1.8, 2.7], write "the displacement grows as √T, consistent with a random walk"; if ≥ 3.5, write "looks more like drift"; otherwise describe it as it is.

## Analysis 2: H1 on the training set (script `代码/train_set_h1.py`)

### Settings
- **Rebuilding the training order**:
  - the subject network and the long lr 1e-4 training generated their minibatch order in the same way: `torch.manual_seed(0)` → build the network (initialization consumes random numbers) → `torch.randperm(15,203,231)`. So the frame order of the two is exactly the same.
  - The subject network saw the first 9,400 × 1,024 = 9,625,600 frames of perm, once each;
  - the step-10,000 checkpoint additionally saw the first 10,240,000 frames of perm again from the beginning.
- **Checking the rebuild**: rerun the first 200 steps of the subject's training from scratch with the rebuilt order, and check that the loss (1.518) and monA (0.5399) at step 200 agree with `日志/train_log.txt`. If they do not agree, stop and report, and do not do the subsequent analysis.
- **Two kinds of training frames** (the three train chunks concatenated in the order 0, 1, 2, the same as in training):
  - **seen**: frames the network saw during training. For the subject, perm[:9,625,600]; for the step-10,000 checkpoint, perm[:10,240,000], of which the first 9,625,600 frames were seen twice.
  - **unseen**: frames of the training set never seen, i.e. the part outside the ranges above. They come from the same utterances and speakers, but these frames themselves never took part in a gradient computation.
  - For each kind, draw 1,000,000 frames with numpy `default_rng(0)` (without replacement).
- **Networks**: subject, H1(subject), H1 without compensation(subject); the step-10,000 checkpoint, H1(it), H1 without compensation(it). H1 is exactly the rule frozen in `heur.py`.
- **Metrics**: accuracy and mean cross-entropy (natural log). In addition, compute the mean cross-entropy of these 6 networks on B and C, so that they can be compared on the same metric (accuracy was reported before and is not recomputed).
- **Uncertainty**: bootstrap by utterance, 2,000 times (rng seed 0). The seen and unseen frames come from the same set of utterances, so both are computed in the same resample, giving an interval for the difference g_seen − g_unseen.

### Predictions of the two explanations
- **Noise explanation** (the far weights are noise that also harms the training loss, but whose gradient signal is too weak to move them): H1 also improves accuracy and lowers the loss on the seen training frames, by an amount similar to that on the unseen frames.
- **Overfitting explanation** (the far weights fit the training data): H1 does not help or harms on the seen frames, and helps on the unseen frames and the test sets, i.e. g_seen is clearly smaller than g_unseen.

### My predictions
- Subject network: each frame was seen at most once, so memorization should be very weak; the subject's own accuracy gap between seen and unseen frames is less than 1 pp.
- H1(subject) improves by about +0.3 to +0.5 pp on the seen frames, with an interval excluding 0; the interval of the difference between g_seen and g_unseen includes 0. The loss decreases as well.
- Step-10,000 checkpoint: H1 is still positive on the seen frames (about +0.1 to +0.2 pp).
- Confidence about seven in ten.

### Decision rules (based mainly on the subject network, H1 with compensation, accuracy; the other three combinations are reported as they are)
- **Supports the noise explanation**: g_seen > 0 with an interval excluding 0, and g_seen ≥ 0.5 × g_unseen.
- **Supports the overfitting explanation**: g_seen ≤ 0 while g_unseen > 0 (interval excluding 0); or g_seen < 0.5 × g_unseen, and the interval of g_seen − g_unseen excludes 0.
- **Other cases**: inconsistent results, reported as they are.

All networks, all combinations and all metrics are reported, without selection.
