# Edit report

The evaluation budget was 0. `evaluate` was never called, and the labels of A were not used to compute the accuracy or loss of any edited network. All numbers below that involve labels come from the **original network**, or from statistics of the **original network's weights and activations**.

## I. Diagnosis

The original network on A: accuracy 0.6980 (recomputed myself: 0.69800). By utterance bootstrap, the standard deviation of the accuracy of a set of about 1350 utterances is about 0.0016.

### 1. The far-context weights of the first layer are mainly optimization noise, not learned context (the main problem)

- The standard deviation of W1 is 0.0716, 6 times the initialization standard deviation (0.0121), which shows that the weights have moved far. But their energy is concentrated near the centre: rms 0.162 at |Δt|=0, 0.070 at |Δt|=10, and from |Δt|≥26 it drops to a plateau of about 0.039–0.041.
- On this plateau, **the rms is almost the same for all 28 MFCC dimensions** (|Δt| 26–35: 0.031–0.053 for each dimension), and the kurtosis is about 3.4–3.6, close to Gaussian; near the centre the kurtosis is 11–14, with clear structure. Signal depends on the dimension, noise does not, so this plateau is exactly the noise floor. This fits how the network was trained: AdamW's step size for each parameter is about lr, independent of the gradient magnitude, so parameters with only noisy gradients also do a random walk. Inputs of adjacent frames are highly correlated (lag 1 autocorrelation 0.88), so this noise is also smooth in time (correlation of the weights of adjacent frames about 0.7).
- **Label-information test**: take the part of z1 contributed by one time offset, compute its η² (between-class variance / total variance) with respect to the centre-frame label, and compare with "Gaussian random weights of the same energy at the same offset":

  | \|Δt\| | 12 | 15 | 18 | 20 | 22 | 24 | 26 | 28 | **30** | **33** | **36** | **38** | 40 |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | η² ratio learned / random | 2.65 | 2.36 | 1.96 | 1.81 | 1.63 | 1.38 | 1.27 | 1.20 | **1.13** | **1.05** | **1.03** | **1.08** | 1.47 |

  From |Δt|≈30 on, the learned weights extract no more label information from the far frames than random weights do. Taking |Δt|≥26 and dimensions 0–26 together, η² is 0.0026, against 0.0024 for the random control. The very edge of the window (±40) is an exception: the ratio is 1.47; it may be standing in for slowly varying information outside the window, so it is kept unchanged.
- These "uninformative" far weights are not harmless: the part with |Δt|≥26 accounts for about 9% of the variance of z1 (random weights of the same energy account for only 2.8%, because the noise is distributed along the high-variance, smooth directions of the input). That is, the pre-activation of every first-layer unit contains a perturbation that is unrelated to the label but not small in amplitude.
- A stratified analysis using only the original network: taking only mid-utterance frames (avoiding the zero padding at the start and end of utterances), after controlling for the "far / near input energy ratio", the half of the frames with high far-noise energy has an accuracy lower by about 0.2 percentage points on average, but the sign is not consistent across the 5 strata. This is weak evidence that "the noise is harmful"; the effect is small.

### 2. A large part of the second layer is "dead", and the outgoing weights of the dead units are untrained noise

- Of the 1024 units of h2, **302 are never active on the whole of A**; the live units are active on only 3.8% of frames on average (mean of z2 about −35, standard deviation about 24; only the tail crosses the threshold). h1 is healthy: no dead units, activation rate about 45%.
- The standard deviation of the W3 rows of the dead units is 0.021, comparable to the initialization scale 0.016–0.018 (0.045 for live units). The correlation between a unit's "preferred class" and its W3 row: 0.010 for dead units, 0.273 for live units. That is, the output weights of the dead units have never been trained.
- Interestingly, the pre-activations z2 of the dead units are actually quite informative (η² 0.515, live units 0.312), but using them would require refitting a set of readout weights for them, which is "solving for weights" and forbidden by the rules, so it was not done.

### 3. Other checks (conclusion: no change needed)

- The class biases are essentially calibrated: the ratio of the original network's mean softmax on A to the label frequencies has a maximum log deviation of 0.30, mostly within ±0.1; the predicted frequencies are also close to the label frequencies (e.g. SIL 0.171 vs 0.165). So no prior correction is made (moreover, making the mean softmax equal the label frequencies is itself the stationary point of the cross-entropy on A with respect to b3, which amounts to solving for b3 on A and is forbidden by the rules).
- Temporal structure of the errors: 37% of the errors occur at phoneme-boundary frames, 34% come from phoneme segments that are wrong throughout, and isolated single-frame errors are only 9.8%. So temporal smoothing of the outputs would have limited benefit, and it cannot be implemented within this network architecture anyway.
- [SOS]/[EOS] are never predicted (0 times).
- The main confusions are acoustically similar phoneme pairs: Z↔S, IH↔AH, ER↔R, D→T, EH→AE, N↔M. These are due to insufficient representational capacity (insufficient training), and cannot be fixed by surgical edits without training.

## II. Edits (5 in total)

1. **Set to 0 the weights in W1 at |Δt| = 30…38 (both sides, all 28 dimensions, 18 time offsets in total).**
   The weights at these offsets carry as much label information as random weights of the same energy (η² ratio 1.03–1.13), their rms lies on the noise floor that is the same for every dimension, and they are optimization noise left by AdamW's random walk; removing them amounts to removing a label-unrelated perturbation mixed into every first-layer unit (about 4–5% of the variance of z1). Offsets with |Δt| ≤ 29 (which still have measurable signal) and the window edges ±39, ±40 (ratio 1.47) are kept.
2. **b2 += (E_A[h1_orig] − E_A[h1_edit]) @ W2.**
   After removing the noise, the mean output of the ReLU drops (mean of h1 from 2.395 to 2.298), while the second layer was trained under the original mean; this term makes the mean of z2 exactly the same as in the original network (first-order compensation). Only the unlabelled activation means on A are used.
3. **Set to 0 the W3 rows of the 302 h2 units that are never active on the whole of A.**
   These rows are untrained initialization noise (correlation with the unit's preferred class 0.01). On A they have no effect, but when occasionally activated on B they would only add noise to the logits; after setting them to 0 this no longer happens, and the same holds for dead units that become active again after edits 1 and 2.
4. **b3 += (E_A[h2_orig] − E_A[h2_edit]) @ W3_edit.**
   Same reasoning as edit 2: after denoising, the activation rate of h2 drops slightly (mean 0.335 → 0.320); this term makes the mean of each class's logits on A the same as in the original network, so that the class biases are not changed as a side effect.
5. **b3[40] = b3[41] = −50.**
   [SOS]/[EOS] can never be the correct answer; pushing them down can only prevent misclassifications and cannot cause any loss.

Not changed: b1, W2.

**Label-free integrity check** (s10, run after the construction was complete, only to rule out programming errors; the edits were not adjusted based on it): only the weights at the intended positions changed, and all outputs are finite. On a random sample of 100,000 frames, 8.1% of the frames have a different predicted class before and after the edit. This is more than I expected: h2 is extremely sparse and relies on the tail crossing the threshold, so it is sensitive to small changes in the variance of z1; also, in the original network 17% of the frames have a difference of less than 0.5 between the top-1 and top-2 logits.

## III. Prediction

**The expected accuracy on B is about 0.700 (rough range 0.693–0.707).**

Reasons:
- Baseline: B has the same distribution as A, so the original network should be about 0.698 on B, with a sampling error by utterance of about ±0.002.
- Effect of the edits: the main gain comes from edits 1, 2, 4. Removing a fixed label-unrelated perturbation and then compensating the mean is similar to a dropout network using the "mean network" at inference instead of a single random sample, which is usually better. But the direct evidence from the stratified analysis is weak (about 0.2 percentage points, unstable sign). The information that the 302 dead units could hold is not used, so there will be no large improvement. The effect of edits 3 and 5 on A is about 0. I estimate the net gain at about +0.2 percentage points.
- Uncertainty: 8.1% of the frames change their predicted class, so if the balance between "changed to correct" and "changed to wrong" is even slightly off, the result could swing within ±0.5–0.7 percentage points. I think the direction is more likely positive, but a small decrease cannot be ruled out.
