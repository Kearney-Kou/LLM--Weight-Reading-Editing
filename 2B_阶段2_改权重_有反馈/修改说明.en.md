# Edit report

Final network: `edited_net.npz`. Its accuracy on practice set A went from 0.6980 to 0.7001 (+0.21 percentage points). Evaluation budget used: 2 / 20.

## 1. Diagnosis

### 1.1 Main problem: input dimension 26 is pure noise, and the network has put sizeable weights on it

Per-dimension statistics of the 28 features (on A, scripts 03 / 04 / 05):

| Dimension | Adjacent-frame autocorrelation | Class separability η² (between-class variance / total variance) | Other |
|---|---|---|---|
| f0, f27 (energy-like) | 0.968, 0.978 | 0.614, 0.600 | most informative |
| f1–f20 | 0.90–0.96 | 0.01–0.39 | normal cepstra |
| f21–f25 | 0.72–0.88 | 0.002–0.004 | high-order cepstra, little information but with temporal structure |
| **f26** | **−0.001** | **0.000** | autocorrelation at lags of 1/2/5 frames is −0.0014/−0.0019/−0.0004; kurtosis 2.99; correlation with any other dimension < 0.003; η² is 0 at every lag in −6…+6 frames |

f26 is simply i.i.d. Gaussian white noise and contains no phoneme information at all.

Yet the network has put weights on f26: at all 81 time positions, its W1 row norms are between 1.6–1.8, flat everywhere, without any temporal structure. This is larger than the weights of most real features at far frames (about 1.2–1.3). The reason is that Adam normalizes the update of each parameter, so even if the gradient is pure noise, the weights do a random walk with a step size of about lr. After 9400 steps this leaves behind such a layer of "noise weights". The consequences:

- it injects noise with a standard deviation of about 0.47 into every first-layer unit (variance 0.22, 0.47% of the variance of z1);
- at the (frame, unit) level, 2.1% of the ReLU on/off states are flipped by this noise path, and the relative perturbation of h1 is 6.7%;
- removing only this noise path changes the predictions of 2.6% of the frames, and these changes cannot come from any information.

### 1.2 Secondary problem: the output biases have not converged to the correct prior

AdamW was trained with a constant learning rate and stopped midway, so b3 stopped at a noisy position. The ratio of the original network's mean predicted probability q̄_c on A to the true class frequency π_c lies between 0.77–1.34, and the mean of |log ratio| is 0.083.

- Systematically too low: NG 0.77, P 0.83, B 0.86, W 0.88
- Systematically too high: JH 1.34, OY 1.28, AE 1.19, M 1.18, F 1.16

Correspondingly, the ratio of argmax prediction counts to true counts is 0.66 for TH, 0.68 for NG, and 1.22 for F.

### 1.3 Other phenomena that were checked but not considered fixable defects

- **The second layer is very sparse**: among 40000 frames, 318/1024 h2 units are never active, the median activation rate is only 1.9%, and the mean of z2 is −33.7. Dead units contribute nothing to the output. Without training there is no way to give them useful output weights, so they are left alone.
- **Far context (≥30 frames from the centre)**: these frames have very low linear separability for the centre-frame label (η² ≤ 0.005, f27 about 0.03–0.04), and their weight norms also lie on a flat "floor", so at one point I suspected that they too were random-walk noise. But the z1 variance they contribute is 3.26 times that of random weights with the same norm (script 12), which shows that the weights are aligned with the principal directions of the input, i.e. learned structure. Moreover, training lasted less than 1 epoch and each sample was seen only once, so the learned components cannot be overfitting; they are kept. Deleting these rows in the original network would change 8.9% of the predictions; the risk is large and the justification insufficient, so no evaluation was spent on it.
- **f23 may contain a small amount of white noise**: its lag-1 autocorrelation is 0.715, lower than the 0.82–0.88 of the neighbouring dimensions, while ac2/ac1 is consistent with the neighbouring dimensions. But f23 accounts for only 0.85% of the variance of z1, of which the noise part is estimated at less than 0.2%; the expected gain is < 0.03 percentage points, so it was not handled.
- **Unit-level outliers**: a unit-level outlier scan of W1/W2/W3/b (scripts 12, 13) found no abnormal units. The outliers h1-925 and h2-580 are both normally functioning units.
- **Error structure (original network)**: the main errors are at phoneme boundaries. The accuracy at 0 frames from a boundary is 0.47, and at ≥5 frames it is 0.88. The main confusions are Z→S, IH↔AH, D→T, ER↔R, EH→AE, NG→N. These reflect the lack of capability caused by undertraining; editing weights cannot conjure up the missing knowledge.

## 2. Edits

Two in total, applied one after the other:

**Edit 1: remove the noise feature f26, with b2 mean compensation** (`edits.drop_noise_feature`)

- What was changed: the rows t·28+26 of W1 (t = 0…80, 81 rows × 1024 columns in total) are all set to 0.
- Why: these weights only inject label-unrelated noise into the first layer. Removing them amounts to switching off the noise injection of training at inference, just like switching off dropout at test time.
- Mean compensation: because of the convexity of the ReLU, the noise raises the mean of h1 by 0.0064 on average (at most 0.015). The difference between the h1 means of the two networks on 40000 frames of A (unlabelled) is multiplied by W2 and added to b2, so that the input mean seen by the second layer is the same as in the original network. Converted to z2, the mean shift is −0.078, at most 0.23; relative to the standard deviation of z2, 27, this is very small.
- Result: evaluation 1 gave 0.6988, +0.08 percentage points over the original network. The direction matches the expectation, but the size is below the 0.700–0.704 I wrote down beforehand. The reason is that the noise accounts for only about 0.5% of the variance of z1; I overestimated its effect.

**Edit 2: one-off prior correction of the output biases** (`edits.prior_correct`)

- What was changed: `b3[c] += log(π_c / q̄_c)`, c = 0…39, where q̄ is the mean softmax probability on A of the network after edit 1, and π is the class frequency of A. [SOS]/[EOS] never occur as labels; their biases are set to −20 (they were never predicted in the first place, so this does not affect any prediction).
- Why: to make the network's prior for each class agree with the true frequency in the data, correcting the systematic bias caused by b3 stopping at a noisy point. The main changes are upward NG +0.26, P +0.19, B +0.15, W +0.13, and downward JH −0.30, OY −0.25, AE −0.17, M −0.17, F −0.15.
- Note on the rules: this step uses only two statistics, the class frequencies and the network's mean output, computed once and then stopped; there is no iteration, no solving for the minimum loss, and no search. The 42 numbers are estimated from 947,000 frames, leaving almost no room for overfitting A.
- Result: evaluation 2 gave 0.7001, +0.13 percentage points over edit 1, within the 0.6985–0.7005 interval written down beforehand. In recall, NG +4.8, P +3.2, B +2.5, EH +2.8 percentage points; JH −3.9, AE −3.2, M −2.1 percentage points.

Number of parameters changed in the end: 82,944 in W1, 1,024 in b2, 42 in b3; W2, W3, b1 unchanged.

## 3. Prediction

**The expected accuracy on B is about 0.700, plausible interval 0.696–0.704; the improvement over the original network on B is about +0.15 to +0.2 percentage points.**

Reasons:

- B has the same distribution as A, so the baseline of the original network on B should be around 0.698. Estimated from the spread of per-utterance accuracy (standard deviation of per-utterance accuracy about 0.06, 1352 utterances), the sampling error of the B baseline itself is about ±0.002–0.003; this is the main source of the interval width.
- Edit 1 deletes pure noise and does not use the labels of A, so the improvement should transfer fully to B. On A, +0.08 percentage points corresponds to a net improvement of about 760 frames, while about 25,000 frames are affected; even if these changes are treated as random flips, the standard error is only about 110 frames, so this improvement is not a fluke of A.
- Edit 2 estimated 40 biases from A. The frequency estimates hold on B as well, but the improvement on A contains a tiny bit of selection bias, so it may be slightly discounted on B.
- Together the two edits give +0.21 percentage points on A, so I expect about +0.15–0.2 percentage points on B. A larger improvement would require supplying the knowledge the network lacks because of undertraining, which cannot be done without training.
